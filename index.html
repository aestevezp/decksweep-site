How it works

Four steps. The Mac does the finding, the phone does the deciding, the Trash does the rest.

1

The Mac scans

A menu-bar helper looks through Desktop, Downloads, Pictures, Documents and Movies — you choose the folders — and ranks what it finds.

2

Pair once

Scan the QR code in that menu with the iPhone app. Phone and Mac talk directly over your Wi‑Fi, encrypted. No account, no cloud.

3

Swipe

Right keeps, left trashes, down decides later. Tap for a full-size preview. Duplicate groups show every copy side by side.

4

Review, then commit

A list shows exactly what will move, with a toggle to change your mind. Then the files go to the Trash. Undo works even after.

See what's reclaimable, by deck

One number for the whole Mac, then decks by kind: screenshots, duplicates, similar photos, large downloads, old installers. Each shows how many files and how much space, and how far you've got.

Decide in a second

Every card says why it's there — "Screenshot · 14 months old" — with the file name, size and folder. Swipe with your thumb or use the buttons; shake to undo.

Duplicates side by side

Exact copies and near-identical bursts come as one card with every copy visible. The one that stays is marked; tap another to keep that one instead.

Nothing moves until you say so

Before anything happens you get the full list with a switch per file. Commit sends them to your Mac's Trash and tells you exactly what moved — and what it left alone.

Built to be safe

Deleting other people's files is a responsibility. These are the rules the Mac helper enforces, not suggestions.

Only the folders you choose

Never system files, never other apps' data, never your Photos library, never anything you didn't point it at.

Trash, never delete

Every file goes to the Trash. Emptying it is a separate step you confirm on the Mac, only within minutes of a commit.

Nothing leaves your Mac

The phone gets small previews; the Mac gets verdicts. No account, no analytics, no third-party code in either app. Privacy policy.

Changed files are left alone

Anything modified after the scan is skipped and reported, so a file you just edited can't be swept by an old verdict.

Install

Two apps, two minutes.

Mac helper

  1. Download DeckSweep-1.0.dmg and open it.
  2. Drag DeckSweepAgent to Applications and open it from there. It's notarized by Apple, so it opens without warnings.
  3. Find ✦ at the top right of your screen. The first scan runs on its own; the menu shows a QR code.
  4. Optional: Launch at login, and Full Disk Access in System Settings for accurate "last opened" dates.

iPhone app

  1. Install DeckSweep from the App Store (link here as soon as it's live).
  2. Open it on the same Wi‑Fi as the Mac and allow Local Network when iOS asks — that's how it finds the Mac.
  3. Tap your Mac, then Scan QR and point the camera at the menu. It reconnects by itself from then on.
  4. No Mac nearby? Try without a Mac runs the whole flow on sample data.

Questions

Short answers; more on the support page.

The phone can't find my Mac

Both on the same Wi‑Fi, ✦ in the Mac's menu bar, and Local Network allowed for DeckSweep in iOS Settings.

Where did the files go?

The Trash on your Mac. Finder → Trash, or Show Trash in the menu. Nothing is deleted until you empty the Trash yourself.

Can I undo?

Shake the phone for the last swipe; use the review list before committing; after committing, the Mac's menu puts files back as long as they're still in the Trash.

Does it touch my Photos library?

No. Photo libraries, apps and other packages are never entered.

Which devices?

macOS 14 Sonoma or later, Apple silicon or Intel. iPhone with iOS 17 or later.
